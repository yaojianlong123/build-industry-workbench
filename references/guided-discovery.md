# Guided discovery

Guide a non-expert through small decisions. Do not present a long questionnaire.

## Conversation sequence

### Stage A: Understand the business

Ask what the user does, who the customers are, what is sold, who works in the business, and where work happens. Offer examples relevant to the named industry.

Summarize: “You serve X by delivering Y through Z.” Let the user correct it.

### Stage B: Reconstruct one real job

Ask the user to describe the most recent typical customer from first contact through payment and after-sales. Probe only missing handoffs, decisions, delays, documents, and tools.

Prefer questions such as “What must you know before you can quote?” over “Which CRM fields do you need?”

### Stage C: Find pain and risk

Ask what gets forgotten, repeated, delayed, disputed, or entered twice. Identify revenue, time, safety, privacy, and customer-trust risks. Rank the top three by frequency and impact.

### Stage D: Define success

Ask what should become easier in the first week and what measurable change would count as success. Convert vague goals into observable outcomes.

### Stage E: Establish constraints

Confirm target software, devices, existing data, collaboration roles, budget/technical comfort, language, accessibility, and any verified regulatory constraints.

## Interaction rules

- Ask no more than five related questions in one message; prefer two or three.
- Explain why a difficult question matters.
- Offer a recommended default and one or two alternatives when choice is useful.
- Allow “不知道”; proceed with a clearly labeled assumption.
- After every stage, give a brief “What I understood” summary.
- Maintain a decision log: confirmed facts, assumptions, open questions, and deferred ideas.
- Do not restart discovery when adapting an existing specification; ask only for differences.

## Requirements conversion

For each pain point, write:

`Current situation -> desired outcome -> capability -> acceptance evidence`

Example:

`Technicians arrive without parts -> improve first-visit completion -> parts-readiness check on each work order -> dispatcher can see every tomorrow job missing a reserved part.`

Prioritize by customer/revenue risk, frequency, time saved, dependency, and setup cost. Keep the initial release small enough for a real user to adopt.
